State Feedback and Observer

English translation

Estimate velocity to control a system that measures only position, and compare estimated velocity and noise sensitivity.

Before practice

01 Can velocity be determined by measuring position?

Description

Position at a single instant is insufficient. Observability concerns whether velocity can be distinguished from a record of changing position under known dynamics.

02 What changes when observer poles move further left?

Description

Initial estimation error may decay faster, but estimator gain for measurement noise may also increase. Compare convergence and noise response together.

1. Can measurements distinguish states?

Starting conditions

With position [m] and velocity [m/s] as states, use ẋ=Ax+Bu\dot x=Ax+Bu, y=Cxy=Cx. Use A=[01−2−3]A=\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\-2&-3\end{bmatrix}, B=[01]B=\begin{bmatrix}0\\1\end{bmatrix}, C=[10]C=\begin{bmatrix}1&0\end{bmatrix}.

Procedure

  1. Form the controllability matrix [B,AB] and observability matrix [C;CA], and find their ranks.
  2. Compare the case where B is a zero vector with the case C=[00]C=\begin{bmatrix}0&0\end{bmatrix}.
  3. Explain the distinction between an ineffective input and an unavailable measurement.

2. Connecting state feedback and observer

Starting conditions

u=−Kx̂+8ru=-K\hat x+8r, r=1r=1. Control poles: −2,−4; observer poles: −8,−10. Use x(0)=[10]x(0)=\begin{bmatrix}1\\0\end{bmatrix}, x̂(0)=[00]\hat x(0)=\begin{bmatrix}0\\0\end{bmatrix}.

Procedure

  1. Calculate K and L, and compare the eigenvalues of A−BKA-BK and A−LCA-LC.
  2. Integrate actual and estimated states simultaneously, and plot position, velocity, and estimation errors over 0–4 s.
  3. Distinguish the reduction in estimation error from the output approaching its reference.

3. Is a faster observer always better?

Starting conditions

Add n(t)=0.02*sin(40*t) to the measurement. Compare observer poles (−8,−10) and (−20,−25), keeping controller K fixed.

Procedure

  1. Use both observer settings in the practice notebook's function, and compare the control input u on the same time axis.
  2. Calculate position-error RMS, velocity-error RMS, and control-input RMS separately after 1 s.
  3. Interpret initial estimation-error convergence separately from the response to measurement noise.

Results to retain

  • Control-input curves, a comparison table of the three RMS values, and justification for the selected setting.

Learning objectives

Decide whether to adjust lead or lag compensation when speed, damping, and steady-state error must be met together.

Baseline plant and lead design point

Starting conditions

G(s)=40s(s+2)G(s)=\frac{40}{s(s+2)}. Targets: ζ=.4\zeta=.4, ωn=10\omega_n=10 rad/s.

Procedure

  1. Calculate the real and imaginary parts of the target complex poles and mark them on the root locus.
  2. Choose the lead compensator's zero and pole, calculate its gain using the angle and magnitude conditions at the target point, and write the compensator expression.
  3. Calculate the distance between the selected point and the actual closed-loop pole.

Results to retain

  • The target point, root-locus plots before and after compensation, and 1 compensator expression.

Checking design objectives with metrics

Starting conditions

Apply a unit step and unit ramp to baseline G and the compensated model from Activity 1. Use 0~5 s and 0~20 s, respectively.

Procedure

  1. Apply the same unit step and unit ramp to the compensator from Activity 1 and the uncompensated model. Use 0–5 s for the step and 0–20 s for the ramp.
  2. Find step overshoot and settling time. For the ramp, plot e(t)=r(t)−y(t)e(t)=r(t)-y(t), obtained by subtracting the output from r(t)=tr(t)=t. Record the final-sample error for each model.

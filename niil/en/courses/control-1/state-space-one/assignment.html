Before practice

01 Information needed for prediction

Example scenario To predict the subsequent motion of two objects at the same position, what must you know besides their current position?

Description
  • You also need the current velocity. If one object is stationary and the other moving, their subsequent positions differ.
  • The constant-acceleration equations also used both starting position and velocity. Now extend them to a model where acceleration depends on position, velocity, and force.

Review related concepts ↗

02 Acceleration and state

With position and velocity as states, must acceleration also be stored separately?

Description
  • In this model, acceleration can be calculated from the current position, velocity, and applied force, so it is not included as a separate state.
  • Velocity is the rate of change of position, and acceleration is the rate of change of velocity. Combining these relationships expresses the 2nd-order differential equation for position as two 1st-order differential equations.

03 States and outputs

Must the information needed to calculate motion always match the quantity being observed?

Description
  • Even when position and velocity are both needed for calculation, the observed output may be position alone.
  • In this exercise, choose position or velocity as the output of the same state model. Distinguish the information retained from the value displayed.
